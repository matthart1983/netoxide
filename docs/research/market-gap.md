# Trust, not Rust, opens the gap

The best-supported gap in November 2026 is a firewall whose stable releases, source and fleet management are all open, built on a typed, transactional config with atomic upgrades and state sync that has been tested. No open project offers that combination today. pfSense and OPNsense charge for fleet management and still run PHP over one schemaless XML file. VyOS has the commit model but sells its stable builds. NethSecurity has the controller and HA but an OpenWrt config model. nfSensei, the Rust rewrite by pfSense's co-founder, has no public source code and one developer. Rust is a sound way to build the control plane, and nothing in the evidence shows buyers asking for it by name. The segment the evidence points to is MSPs and multi-site IT teams that already run pfSense or OPNsense fleets, with EU buyers under the Cyber Resilience Act as the second ring. Three caveats matter for a product spec. Netgate's 26.10 merger, announced 29 October 2026, removes "pfSense is closing down" as a pitch. The evidence does not support displacing Fortinet or Palo Alto on threat inspection. And segment size, buyer willingness to pay, and every performance comparison rest on thin or missing data. I mark those gaps throughout.

## Netgate's 26.10 reversal removes the easy pitch against pfSense

For five years the story that sent users to OPNsense was that Netgate was closing pfSense. Netgate split off closed-source pfSense Plus in January 2021, ended the free Home+Lab licence in October 2023 ([Netgate blog](https://www.netgate.com/blog/addressing-changes-to-pfsense-plus-homelab)), and in 2025 shipped CE 2.8.0 only through an online installer tied to a store account ([Netgate docs](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/install/download-installer-image.html)). CE went about 18 months between 2.7.2 and 2.8.0 while Plus shipped ten releases ([Netgate version table](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/releases/versions.html)).

On **29 October 2026** Netgate reversed. From release 26.10, promised before the end of November, there is one edition with every feature free on amd64, no account, an offline installer, a built-in API, a new GUI and free security fixes. Netgate now charges for online version upgrades, fleet management and support ([Netgate, "pfSense at 20"](https://www.netgate.com/blog/pfsense-at-20)). This is well sourced as an announcement. It is not yet a shipped product, and every 26.10 detail here is a promise.

Two frictions survive the merger, and both are documented. Free users move to each new version by reinstalling, and the reinstall reformats the disk and erases ZFS boot environments, according to a Netgate employee quoted in the press ([IT-Connect](https://www.it-connect.tech/pfsense-26-10-unifies-ce-and-plus-making-all-features-free/)). Netgate has also not said what licence the merged edition carries ([it-daily.net](https://www.it-daily.net/?p=192829)). Add three policy reversals in under six years and the remaining weakness is trust. Users on the Lawrence Systems forum said so in August 2026, before the merger: "you never know if negate do something which end ups creating a headache across your installed base" ([Lawrence Systems forum](https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/t/the-state-of-pfsense-in-2026-youtube-release/27009)). That thread is one forum with a handful of posters. Reddit returned errors to every fetch, so the largest pfSense and OPNsense communities are unread.

### The architecture limits are documented, the performance gap is not

Both products descend from m0n0wall. PHP pages write a single config.xml and regenerate service configs. OPNsense has spent twelve years moving pages to an MVC and API layer and was still migrating outbound NAT in 26.7 ([OPNsense architecture docs](https://docs.opnsense.org/development/architecture.html); [26.7 notes](https://docs.opnsense.org/releases/CE_26.7.html)). A Netgate engineer wrote that many new pfSense API endpoints "can be confusing (because config.xml is schemaless)" ([Netgate forum](https://forum.netgate.com/post/1225337)). Terraform and Ansible support on both platforms comes from community projects that are pre-1.0 ([OPNsense provider](https://github.com/browningluke/terraform-provider-opnsense); [pfSense provider](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/marshallford/terraform-provider-pfsense@v0.7.2)).

The vendor docs themselves state the hard limits. pfSense supports exactly two nodes in an HA cluster, and pfsync has no authentication ([Netgate HA docs](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/highavailability/index.html); [HA components](https://docs.netgate.com/pfsense/en/latest/solutions/reference/highavailability/components.html)). FreeBSD's packet processing defaults to one thread, RSS is off by default, and OPNsense marks only two NIC drivers as tested with it. The same page says Suricata's netmap mode re-injects on one thread ([OPNsense performance docs](https://docs.opnsense.org/troubleshooting/performance.html)). Fleet management costs money on both sides. Netgate Nexus is licensed per managed device per year and excludes air-gapped systems ([Nexus FAQ](https://www.netgate.com/support/frequently-asked-questions-nexus)). OPNcentral ships only in the Business Edition ([Thomas-Krenn wiki](https://www.thomas-krenn.com/en/wiki/Additional_features_of_the_OPNsense_Business_Edition)).

OPNsense's fast cadence has a cost. The 26.1 and 26.7 lines produced forum threads on broken DHCP migration, Kea failures, Hyper-V kernel panics and a reverted gateway change ([26.1 thread](https://forum.opnsense.org/index.php?topic=50688.0); [26.7.7 thread](https://forum.opnsense.org/index.php?topic=53145.new)). Users on the same Lawrence Systems thread asked for "a more conservative LTS type version".

Security history points the same way, with a caveat on sourcing. Netgate's 2026 web GUI advisories are numbered at least to SA-26_21 and cover stored XSS ([SA-26_21](https://docs.netgate.com/downloads/pfSense-SA-26_21.webgui.asc); [SA-26_11](https://docs.netgate.com/downloads/pfSense-SA-26_11.webgui.asc)). Aggregator sites also list command injection and privilege escalation from limited GUI roles to root in both products, plus OPNsense entries rated 9.1 ([vuln.today](https://vuln.today/tag/opnsense)). Nobody checked the aggregator entries against NVD, so treat them as leads. The recurring bug class is PHP interpolating input into shell commands and HTML. Memory safety does not fix that. A design with no shell-outs, typed config and templated output does.

Performance is where I would not let the spec make claims. The notes found no current, independent benchmark of pfSense or OPNsense against Linux at 10G or above. The evidence is vendor figures, one lab test from around 2019 and forum anecdotes.

## Each open rival holds half of the product

The rest of the open field splits into projects with a good config model and projects with a complete feature list. None has both.

| Product | Stable release open and free | Transactional config | Fleet management | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pfSense 26.10 (announced) | Features free, licence unstated | No, schemaless XML | Paid per device, no air-gap | Free users reinstall to upgrade |
| OPNsense | Yes | No, XML with partial MVC | Paid Business Edition | Regressions in 2026 releases |
| VyOS | No, LTS is subscriber-only | Yes, but rollback reboots | Not confirmed in notes | No IDS/IPS or web UI focus |
| NethSecurity | Yes, with paid enterprise repo | Inherits OpenWrt UCI (inferred) | Yes, first-party Controller | API story not documented in notes |
| nfSensei | No public source | Claimed, behind beta login | Labs feature | One developer, lab-tested only |

**VyOS** is the strongest open router and a weak firewall product. Since 1.4, LTS images, source and package repositories go to subscribers only, and in June 2024 the maintainers closed the LTS repos permanently ([VyOS forum announcement](https://forum.vyos.io/t/lts-release-package-repositories-permanently-closed-for-public-access/14637)). VyOS 1.5 LTS shipped on 31 March 2026 with an optional VPP data plane and a vendor claim of "up to 15x" kernel forwarding ([VyOS blog](https://blog.vyos.io/vyos-1.5.0-ga-release)). Its commit-confirm reboots the box when the timer expires ([VyOS docs](https://docs.vyos.io/en/1.2/commandtree/configmode.html)), and users have asked for a soft rollback ([VyOS T5976](https://phabricator.vyos.net/T5976)). Published prices conflict across VyOS's own pages and could not be verified. The claims that VyOS lacks any IDS/IPS and gave subscribers security fixes five weeks early come from one third-party review ([Mr.PlanB](https://www.mrplanb.com/directory/firewalls/vyos)) and need confirmation.

**NethSecurity** is the closest thing to what a new open firewall would look like. Version 8.8.0 shipped on 29 July 2026 on OpenWrt 25.12.5 with DPI, IPsec, OpenVPN, WireGuard, HA since 8.6 and a central Controller, with a paid enterprise repository ([NethServer community](https://community.nethserver.org/t/nethsecurity-8-8-0-geoblocking-alerts-metrics-emergency-cli-tool/28170); [nethsecurity.org](https://nethsecurity.org/)). A new entrant has to beat it on something concrete. The notes did not capture its licence name, API or prices, so the comparison is incomplete.

Elsewhere the field is thin. IPFire releases monthly but its 2026 work is DNS filtering ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/28/open-source-ipfire-dns-firewall/)). DynFi, the only OPNsense fork found, last released in March 2023 ([DynFi releases](https://dynfi.com/en/dynfi-products/firewall/dynfi-firewall-releases/)). The real pull on small sites comes from closed, cheap hardware. A UniFi Cloud Gateway Ultra lists at 129 USD with IDS/IPS ([Ubiquiti store](https://store.ui.com/us/en/category/all-unifi-cloud-gateways/products/ucg-ultra)), and forum threads show pfSense users and MSPs moving to it ([Lawrence Systems thread](https://forums.lawrencesystems.com/t/suggestion-is-unifi-ucg-up-to-the-task-to-move-from-pfsense-in-2026/26791)). The notes found no "Talos for firewalls", meaning an immutable image with one declarative config and API-only management. That absence rests on one search pass.

### nfSensei has the name and the feature list, not the code

Scott Ullrich's nfSensei has claimed the "pfSense done right, in Rust" position. Its public material lists three data planes, FRR routing, Suricata IPS, VRRP and conntrackd HA, an SD-WAN mesh, a pfSense config importer and candidate config with confirmed commits ([nfsensei.org](https://nfsensei.org/)). The facts that matter for a competitor are well sourced, because the researcher read the pages and APIs directly on 14 November 2026.

There is **no public source**. The GitHub organisation has zero public repositories ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/orgs/nfSensei)), and docs and images return HTTP 401 without beta credentials. The homepage says AGPL-3.0-or-later with a commercial licence, while an older page on the same site still says "BSD licensed" ([nfsensei.org/supporttheproject](https://nfsensei.org/supporttheproject)). The project is one self-funded person with no named company. It shipped 45 releases in 30 days with 718 fix commits, and its own notes disclose that HA state sync "wasn't actually replicating state" until 1 October 2026 and that credentials were unmasked in exports before 0.51.218 ([nfSensei blog](https://blog.nfsensei.org/thirty-days-of-shipping.html)). Its stated audience is home labs, and it publishes no support plans.

Public attention is small. The best Hacker News thread reached 19 points, and the criticism there was about AI-written prose ([HN 48871758](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48871758)). The "over 150 testers" figure comes from a search snippet of a page that returned 403, so I would not quote it. The inference that AI agents write much of the code is the researcher's own, drawn from the release rate and one HN comment by Ullrich.

The only other Rust project with users is Landscape Router, GPL-3.0 with about 1,600 stars and a release on 13 November 2026. It installs on an existing distro and targets per-domain traffic steering for home users ([Landscape README](https://github.com/ThisSeanZhang/landscape/blob/main/README.md)). Everything else found is days old or a single component. The search was GitHub keywords only, so projects on GitLab or Codeberg could be missing.

## Commercial pain shows up in vendor documents, rarely in buyers' words

The complaint evidence against commercial NGFW vendors is strongest where the vendor wrote it down. Under Meraki's co-termination licensing, devices stop passing client traffic once an organisation is out of compliance past the 30-day grace period ([Meraki docs](https://documentation.meraki.com/General_Administration/Licensing/License_Problem_-_Out_of_Compliance)). A FortiGate with an expired contract cannot upgrade to a higher major or minor FortiOS version ([Fortinet docs](https://docs.fortinet.com/document/fortigate/7.4.0/new-features/299518)). Fortinet removed SSL VPN tunnel mode in FortiOS 7.6.3 and does not carry the configuration over on upgrade ([Fortinet community tip](https://community.fortinet.com/fortigate-3/technical-tip-changes-on-ssl-vpn-modes-starting-from-v7-6-3-204117)). These three are primary and safe to cite. An open firewall can answer each with a written policy that it never stops forwarding and never withholds a security fix.

Pricing evidence is weak. A FortiGate 60F with one year of UTP lists between about 835 and 1,190 USD depending on reseller ([datacenter360](https://datacenter360.ca/shop/network-security/firewall/bundle/fortigate-60f-hardware-plus-1-year-forticare-premium-and-fortiguard-unified-threat-protection-utp-fg-60f-bdl-950-12/); [CDW](https://www.cdw.com/product/fortinet-fortigate-60f-security-appliance-with-1-year-forticare-premium/5827216)). The renewal-hike percentages in circulation come from aggregator sites and could not be traced to a customer or a vendor. Leave them out of the spec. The MSP evidence is vendors describing each other, such as WatchGuard on Fortinet's separate consoles and Sophos on per-device patching at SonicWall. No MSP speaks in their own words in the notes.

The security record is solid. VulnCheck counted **181 exploited network edge device vulnerabilities in 2025**, 102 of them in enterprise products and only 23.7% listed in CISA KEV, with seven tied to ransomware on Fortinet, SonicWall, Ivanti and Citrix devices ([VulnCheck report](https://wwv.vulncheck.com/hubfs/Research/Exploring-Network-Edge-Devices-VulnCheck-State-of-Exploitation-2026.pdf)). SonicWall admitted attackers reached configuration backups for every customer of its cloud backup service ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/09/sonicwall-urges-password-resets-after.html)). That makes mandatory vendor cloud a liability, and self-hosted management an argument.

What the record does not give is a number for memory safety. Horizon3 found memory-safety issues were about 20% of 2023 KEV additions across all products ([Horizon3](https://www.horizon3.ai/?p=242945)). No published figure covers firewalls and VPN gateways alone. The well-known pre-auth exploits split between memory corruption in C daemons and command injection or authentication bypass, such as PAN-OS CVE-2024-3400, which CISA and the FBI named in their 2024 alert on command injection ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2024/07/10/cisa-and-fbi-release-secure-design-alert-eliminating-os-command-injection-vulnerabilities)). Rust removes the first class and leaves the second to design.

The market is growing. Dell'Oro reports firewall revenue growth of 9% in 1Q 2026 and **18% in 2Q 2026**, in a network security market near 8 billion USD a quarter, driven by "aging infrastructure, higher throughput requirements, branch redesigns, and expanding private-cloud workloads" ([Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/newsroom/firewall-growth-doubles-to-18-percent-in-2q-2026-on-refresh-and-capacity-expansion/)). SASE grows faster, above 20% for five straight quarters ([Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/news/sase-revenue-grows-above-20-percent-as-sd-wan-reaccelerates-in-2q-2026/)). Remote-user inspection is moving to the cloud, and the on-prem job that remains is branch connectivity, site throughput and segmentation. Absolute market-size figures from research resellers disagree by a factor of eight and should not appear in the spec. No SMB or MSP sizing exists in the notes.

## The CRA turns any seller of a firewall into a Class II manufacturer

The EU Cyber Resilience Act is the regulation with teeth for this product. Annex III lists firewalls and intrusion detection and prevention systems as Important products, Class II ([Annex III](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/annex-3/)). Class II requires a notified body, with no self-assessment ([Finite State](https://finitestate.io/blog/conformity-assessments-eu-cra-requirements)). Manufacturer reporting duties began on **11 September 2026** and the regulation applies in full from **11 December 2027** ([OpenSSF](https://policy.openssf.org/CRA/stewards-one-pager.html)). Manufacturers owe a declared support period of at least five years in most cases ([Article 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/)) and a machine-readable SBOM.

The Act splits an open-source firewall into two legal roles. A steward, which must be a legal entity and can be a company, carries light duties and is exempt from administrative fines ([heise](https://heise.de/-11177697); [OpenSSF](https://policy.openssf.org/CRA/stewards-one-pager.html)). Whoever sells appliances or a paid build is a manufacturer with the full set. The spec should plan the commercial entity around that cost from the start. The notes hold no sourced cost or duration for a Class II assessment, and nobody read the regulation text or the Commission's July 2026 guidance directly. Get legal review before the spec relies on steward status.

In the United States the pressure is on end-of-life and forensic visibility. CISA's Binding Operational Directive 26-02 of 5 February 2026 orders federal civilian agencies to inventory and remove end-of-support edge devices, with deadlines that secondary sources report differently ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/02/06/cisa-orders-us-federal-agencies-to-replace-unsupported-edge-devices/)). Emergency Directive 25-03 covered compromised Cisco ASA devices ([CISA](https://cisa.gov/news-events/directives/ed-25-03-identify-and-mitigate-potential-compromise-cisco-devices)). The Five Eyes edge-device guidance of February 2025 asks manufacturers for secure logging by default and tamper-resistant log storage ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/five-eyes-agencies-release-guidance-on-securing-edge-devices/)). Those read as product requirements, and an open, inspectable OS meets them cheaply. NSA and CISA urge memory-safe languages in guidance from June 2025 ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2025/06/24/new-guidance-released-reducing-memory-related-vulnerabilities)). That guidance is advisory. The notes found no binding federal rule requiring memory-safe languages.

Federal sales have their own gate. FIPS 140-2 certificates went historical on 21 September 2026, so new acquisitions need an active 140-3 module ([SafeLogic](https://www.safelogic.com/blog/what-happens-on-september-21-2026)). OPNsense Business Edition holds Spain's LINCE certification by Deciso's account ([Deciso](https://www.deciso.com/opnsense-business-edition-25-10-elevating-enterprise-control-with-smarter-security-seamless-identity-and-scheduled-automation/)). The researcher found no FIPS or NDcPP listing for pfSense or OPNsense, which is an absence in one search and not proof.

## Rust fits the control plane, while the packet path stays in the kernel

The technical research is clear on what Rust can carry today. It is a userspace control plane, eBPF programs through Aya, and netlink or JSON calls into nftables. It is not an in-kernel data plane. Mainline Linux dropped the "experimental" label for Rust in 7.0 ([Linux Pro Magazine](https://www.linuxpromagazine.com/index.php/layout/set/print/Online/News/Kernel-7.0-Is-a-Bit-More-Rusty)), but the networking Rust found in mainline is PHY-level ([Phoronix](https://phoronix.com/news/Linux-6.8-Rust-PHY-Driver)). Aya is still 0.x and is working to get Rust's eBPF targets to Tier 2 so programs build on stable Rust ([FOSDEM 2026](https://fosdem.org/2026/schedule/event/aya)).

| Component | Rust option | Maturity per sources | Sensible fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| TLS and FIPS crypto | rustls with aws-lc-rs | Production, FIPS 140-3 certificates active | None needed |
| NTP | ntpd-rs | Stable, packaged, deployed at Let's Encrypt | chrony |
| HTTP proxy | Pingora | Production at Cloudflare | None needed |
| WireGuard | BoringTun and forks | Three teams forked it, one abandoned the fork | Kernel WireGuard |
| DNS recursion | Hickory | Crate page says not recommended for production | Unbound |
| DHCP | dora | Self-described early release | Kea |
| Routing | Holo | 0.9, small team, NLnet-funded | FRR |
| IDS/IPS | none | Suricata 8 is C plus Rust with library mode | Embed Suricata |
| Packet filter | nftables crates | Small crates, one Apache-2.0, one GPL-3.0+ | libnftables |

Sources for the table are the rustls FIPS manual and NIST listings ([rustls](https://docs.rs/rustls/0.23.31/rustls/manual/_06_fips/); [NIST #5429](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/cryptographic-module-validation-program/certificate/5429)), Trifecta Tech on ntpd-rs ([Trifecta](https://trifectatech.org/projects/ntpd-rs)), the Hickory crate page ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/hickory-dns/latest)), the dora repository ([GitHub](https://github.com/bluecatengineering/dora)), Holo's releases ([GitHub](https://github.com/holo-routing/holo/releases)) and OISF's FOSDEM slides on Suricata 8 ([OISF](https://fosdem.org/2026/events/attachments/YCYWGM-suricata/slides/266795/suricata8_4qjn5lu.pdf)). The fallback column is the researcher's recommendation. Most of this section came from search summaries, and only the Aya and Holo release pages were opened directly.

Proxmox is the calibration point for schedule. It posted a Rust nftables replacement for its Perl firewall in April 2024 ([pve-devel](https://lists.proxmox.com/pipermail/pve-devel/2024-April/062511.html)). In June 2026 a forum thread still quoted the docs as calling it a tech preview unsuitable for production, and staff gave "no general recommendation" ([Proxmox forum](https://forum.proxmox.com/threads/proxmox-9-and-nftables-status.184051/)). A funded vendor with a narrow scope needed more than two years and has not reached parity. Reaching parity with an incumbent rule engine is the slow part. Pingora's three request-smuggling CVEs in 2026 make the companion point that Rust does not prevent protocol-logic bugs ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/pingora-oss-smuggling-vulnerabilities)).

Two technical openings have real sources behind them. The first is HA. Linux state sync means conntrackd plus keepalived, which is asynchronous and has documented races and failures in asymmetric setups ([netfilter list](https://spinics.net/lists/netfilter/msg60848.html)). pfsync has a `defer` mode that holds the first packet until a peer acknowledges the state ([OpenBSD pfsync](https://man.openbsd.org/pfsync)), and conntrackd has no equivalent. A netfilter maintainer advises against active/active with asymmetric routing ([netfilter list](https://spinics.net/lists/netfilter/msg58576.html)), so a 1.0 should commit to active/backup. The second is hardware. FreeBSD needs out-of-tree drivers for Realtek 2.5GbE and Intel E610 ([Netgate forum](https://forum.netgate.com/topic/198287/e610-xt2)), and users report virtualising pfSense to borrow Linux drivers ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47576017)). MediaTek hardware flow offload on Arm boards is a Linux-only path, though the throughput figures come from a vendor-affiliated blog and the Banana Pi forum reports corruption bugs.

The notes found no same-hardware benchmark comparing nftables flowtable, XDP, pf and VPP. Any performance table in the spec would stitch together sources from 2017 to 2026 on different NICs. One finding does translate into a requirement. A basic university benchmark saw throughput fall to 250 kpps at 1,000 linear rules ([uv.es](https://www.uv.es/cuan/vyos_bsdrp/)), so the rule compiler must emit sets and maps.

## Open features and paid convenience is the model with precedent

Both incumbents price the software near zero and sell the same things. TAC Lite costs 129 USD a year ([Netgate blog](https://www.netgate.com/blog/netgate-pfsense-plus-tac-lite-available-for-129-per-year)) and OPNsense Business Edition 149 EUR a year ([Deciso shop](https://shop.opnsense.com/product/opnsense-business-edition/)), next to appliances from roughly 549 USD to several thousand. A new entrant cannot win on licence price. The paid items are hardware, support and fleet management, and after 26.10 Netgate adds upgrade convenience to that list. Proxmox sells a stable repository per CPU socket ([Proxmox](https://www.proxmox.com/proxmox-ve/pricing)) and Sidero Labs sells a management plane over an open OS. No revenue figure for Netgate, Deciso, VyOS or Proxmox is public, and the two Deciso estimates found differ by fifteen times, so the notes contain no evidence about how large such a business gets.

Relicensing after adoption has a consistent record. HashiCorp's move to BSL produced OpenTofu, Redis's change produced Valkey, and Elastic's produced OpenSearch before both companies added AGPL ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/what-happens-to-relicensed-open-source-projects-and-their-forks/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/29/elastic-founder-on-why-they-returned-to-open-source-four-years-after-going-proprietary)). The pfSense audience still voices its 2023 grievance three years on. VyOS's closed LTS repos spawned rebuild projects. The lesson for the spec is to pick the licence once and bind it through governance, for example a steward entity holding the trademark with no contributor agreement that permits relicensing. nfSensei's quiet move from BSD to AGPL plus commercial terms, with the code still private, is the opposite approach.

Grants exist for memory-safe infrastructure but fund components. Prossimo's list covers Rustls, Hickory, ntpd-rs and sudo, with no firewall or packet-filter initiative ([memorysafety.org](https://www.memorysafety.org/blog/2026-craig-newmark/)). NLnet's final NGI Zero Commons Fund call closed on 1 June 2026, with grants of 5,000 to 50,000 EUR ([NLnet](https://www.nlnet.nl/commonsfund)). The Sovereign Tech Agency lists its fund as open ([STA](https://sovereign.tech/programs/fund)). A Rust nftables library or state-sync daemon fits their pattern. A firewall product does not. Venture money in adjacent networking went to hosted control planes, such as NetBird's 10 million USD Series A in January 2026 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/01/13/netbird-announces-10m-series-a-to-expand-open-source-vpn-alternative/)).

## The gap is verified, open fleet operations for existing pfSense shops

Putting the strands together, the single best-supported gap is this. Nobody ships a firewall where the stable release and its source are public and every feature is free, where config is typed, versioned data with commit-confirm and rollback that does not reboot, and where a first-party fleet controller with role separation and air-gap support sits in the open core. The same product would need signed image upgrades that roll back on a failed boot, and HA whose state sync has been tested in the field. Each piece answers a documented fault. Schemaless config.xml, paid and cloud-tied fleet tools, reinstall-to-upgrade, two-node unauthenticated HA, VyOS's rebooting rollback and nfSensei's unreadable code are all sourced above. Rust serves this as the implementation language of a control plane that never shells out. The buyer sees it only through what the product can show for it, meaning public source, a published config schema and a cargo-generated SBOM.

The target segment follows from who feels those faults. It is MSPs and multi-site IT teams running tens to low hundreds of pfSense or OPNsense boxes, who want automation and resent per-device management fees. Evidence for this is moderate. Both incumbents concentrate paid work on fleet management, a Netgate forum user evaluating Nexus for about 100 firewalls asked for finer RBAC ([Netgate forum](https://forum.netgate.com/post/1245184)), a third-party SaaS sells OPNsense central management ([NetDefense](https://netdefense.io/opnsense-central-management)), and users report features between pfSense and OPNsense as "essentially the same", so lock-in is habit and config migration. A config importer is therefore a launch requirement, as Sophos shows with its FortiGate migration tool. EU organisations facing CRA and supply-chain questionnaires are the second ring, where an EU steward and a certification path matter. Deciso is Dutch and already holds LINCE, so being European is not enough by itself.

Timing favours a start now, with the product arriving into three dated events. Free pfSense users meet their first reinstall-to-upgrade cycle after 26.10. BOD 26-02 pushes end-of-support edge devices out of US federal networks. CRA applies in full in December 2027.

Several things the evidence does not support, and the spec should not assume them. It does not show demand for Rust as such. What users ask for is a Linux base, a real API and independence from Netgate. It does not support a pitch that memory safety would have prevented the pfSense and OPNsense bug record, which is injection and XSS. It does not support rivalling Fortinet or Palo Alto on application identification and threat intelligence. Those need a paid content pipeline, and the notes hold no sourced data on enterprise checklist items such as TLS inspection, ZTNA, SIEM integration or compliance reporting. It does not support a pure-Rust services stack at 1.0, since DNS, DHCP and routing in Rust are pre-1.0 by their maintainers' own description. It does not support any performance claim against pf or VPP. It does not support EU public-sector demand for a firewall specifically. The sovereignty money found buys workplace software, no tender for an open-source firewall turned up, and one headline says ambitions "stall at the procurement desk" ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366645394/Europes-sovereignty-ambitions-stall-at-the-procurement-desk)). It does not support grant funding for the product as a whole. And it says nothing about demand for an AI assistant, where the only reaction found was a forum poster hoping nfSensei's is optional.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "A Rust firewall that rivals pfSense" describes nfSensei, and nfSensei shows what that framing produces. Its feature list grew faster than anyone could verify it, and outsiders cannot read the code. The opening for a second entrant is the opposite posture, with a narrower scope and public code from the first commit, where the claims are the ones a buyer can check. Those are the licence and governance, the config schema, the upgrade and rollback behaviour, the HA test results and the support period. Netgate's merger makes this sharper. Once features are free everywhere, the remaining differences are who controls the upgrade path and the fleet, and whether the vendor's promises are binding.

Before the spec hardens, three pieces of missing evidence are worth buying. Read Reddit's pfSense, OPNsense and MSP communities, which this research could not reach, to test whether fleet operators say in their own words what vendor marketing says for them. Run a same-hardware benchmark, because the spec will otherwise inherit numbers nobody can defend. Get a legal reading of CRA Class II cost and steward status, since that decides the shape of the company as much as the product. Then check what 26.10 ships with and under what licence, because that release sets the baseline every claim here is measured against.
